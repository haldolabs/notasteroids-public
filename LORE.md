# The Consciousness of Stone

Everything below is in the game. None of it is required reading — you can fly the rock
without any of it, and the game never stops to explain itself.

---

## You

You are a **Lithonaut**. Not a pilot in a ship: a **tardigrade-fungal collective** living
inside a shell of sculpted stone, flying it the way you would fly your own body, because
it is.

The idea came from a real one — that carbonaceous asteroids have the chemistry, the water
and the time for something to happen inside them. In the game's version it did happen,
emerging over generations in thermal oases, and it went on happening quietly for a long
time before anything noticed.

**A rock does not die.** It goes to spore state, or it is **lost** — mislaid, discarded,
downhill. Whatever the mechanics do to you, that is the word for it.

## The Song

The Song is in the stone. The tunnel sings. The hum goes on longer than the light does.

Nothing in the game says where it started, and nothing should — *we do not know exactly
when that first started* is better than an origin.

## The Axiom

The Axiom believes the universe is a spreadsheet with rendering errors, and is working
through them. It is not cruel. It is **tidying**.

What you meet out in the systems was never the Axiom itself — only its fingers. It keeps
its home somewhere distance does not apply to.

---

## The five legs

### 1 — The star map

The war, and the part with the hours in it. Ten systems. You take them one at a time until
there are none left, and each one has a boss that has a personality about it.

### 2 — The road

Taking the map does not end anything; it opens something.

The way to the Axiom's home is a vessel threaded through several universes at once, built
by something that does not count in miles. It sings, because everything does.

### 3 — The horizon

The road ends at **three black holes orbiting each other**, with the Axiom's central mind
sitting in the burst of everything falling past them, running on it.

You are close enough that the tide has rolled your hull flat.

That is why one game in the collection is played on a plane, with a paddle-shaped you. And
if you want the whole thing: it is why a game about rocks in space is drawn in two
dimensions with a vector beam at all. Near enough to that horizon, everything is.

*(The line was free. The experiment's own menu blurb had said THE HULL IS ROLLED FLAT long
before there was a story for it to be true in.)*

### 4 — The iron

You win, and what is left is you: a sentient rock in an open field, gathering other rocks,
cooling.

Accreting makes you pull harder. The only way to put the load down is to let go of it.
Nobody says that out loud and nobody should — matter, some of which is sentient, tells
space how to curve, and space tells matter how to move.

At the end there is a cold iron sphere, and one stroke that did not go cold.

### S — The trash

Every loss on every leg falls here, among everything the Axiom ever threw away. Nobody
thinks about where the trash goes.

Hold out and it puts you back where you fell. Break up in here and the run is over.

It is not a punishment. It is only where things end up.

## Voice

The game is dry rather than jokey, and it does not write arcade filler. There is no
"GAME OVER, TRY AGAIN!" anywhere in it.

You can also **change who is telling you all this**. Narration voices are swappable, and
a voice may *notice* things about your run — and may be **wrong** about them, if you turn
that on. A voice can bend a number or spin a conclusion.

Two rules that distortion follows, because an unreliable narrator nothing can contradict
is just a bug:

* **The seed is exempt.** A wrong seed is a broken feature, not a joke.
* **The truth still reaches the run record.** Whatever a voice tells you, the honest
  version is still there to be checked.
